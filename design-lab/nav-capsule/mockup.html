The Launch Cradle

One floating capsule instead of three evenly spaced slots. A shallow cut in its top edge seats the rocket like a key; the selected destination is a well pressed into the plate. The dock keeps the 72 dp footprint of the bar it replaces, so nothing moves closer to the picture.

One object,
one key.

The old bar was a strip with two tabs and a ball floating above it. This is a single machined plate: two recessed seats and one raised key. It reads as one thing at arm's length, and the rocket is special because of where it sits, not how loud it is.

1

The plate

252 × 54 dp, Surface #151922 with a 1 dp BorderStrong hairline and a soft 10 dp shadow. It floats 8 dp above the navigation inset, clear of the gesture pill.

2

The well

Selection is a 72 × 40 dp seat cut into the plate — the Background colour with an inset rim, not a bright fill. The well slides; the labels never move.

3

The key

A 44 dp disc seated in a 58 dp cradle, lifted 8 dp. SurfaceStrong with an accent glyph. One tap sends the rocket; the plate never changes size.

What it replaces — 72 dp, three slots, one accent ball

Today the bar is a full-width opaque strip — Feed · oversized accent ball · Archive — with the rocket raised 16 dp out of it on a second 80 dp ring. It works, but the accent surface makes the random action the loudest thing on every screen, and the strip cuts the picture with a hard edge. The capsule trades the ball for a key and lets the feed's own gradient end on the app background, so the picture has no visible bottom edge.

The well is Background, so it reads as a hole rather than a highlight. The key is SurfaceStrong, the lightest surface in the dock, so the eye finds it before the labels. Accent is used once: the rocket glyph. The selected label simply becomes Text + SemiBold.

States & type scaling

static frames · motion is specified in compose-notes.md

Motion, quietly

Only two things ever move: the well and the key's ring. Nothing scales on selection and no ripple is used.

Selection · 220 msWell slides 148 dp on FastOutSlowIn. Icon + label crossfade 120 ms; label weight swaps at the same time.
Press · 90 ms downKey scales to 0.96 with a light haptic. Release springs back in 220 ms; the ring stays muted.
Launch · 300 msAn accent ring opens 44 → 58 dp and fades. The random dialog then takes over the screen.
04

Stress tests

the three cases most likely to break a floating dock

320 dp · narrow

34 dp outside margins. The plate does not shrink, so both cells keep their 84 dp width and the key stays at the exact centre.

3-button navigation

The 48 dp system bar joins the slot: 120 dp reserved. The plate still floats 8 dp above the buttons and clears them; only the picture area gets shorter.

Landscape · 640 × 360

A compact build: 44 dp plate, 36 dp key, 11 sp labels, 8 dp from the bottom edge. Band is 80 dp (22% of height) instead of 96 dp. Under 320 dp of height, a side rail is the next step — not v1.

05

Also considered

kept in the lab, not recommended

B · Split dock

Two islands with the launch key floating in the bay between them, a 1 dp rail joining the key to the baseline. It separates browse from library very clearly, but costs 4 dp of footprint and reads as three objects again — the exact complaint that started this round.

Rejected · three objects, taller band

C · Launch key

An asymmetric plate: two quieter destinations on the left, a sealed launch bay on the right behind a 1 dp hairline. Best thumb reach for the action, but it demotes the rocket from the midpoint, fights the feed's right-edge action rail, and weakens the "two equal destinations" story.

Rejected · wrong hierarchy, rail collision
